How Consumer Trends Could Reshape the Retail Sector in 2027.

An evidence-led exploration of changing consumer priorities, retail economics, and the commercial decisions that may matter in 2027.

INTRODUCTION / 00

Understanding the retail landscape.

Consumer trends in 2027 could reshape how retailers attract customers, manage product ranges, and protect profitability. Financial caution, AI-assisted shopping, and changing lifestyle priorities will influence purchasing decisions, but their commercial effects will depend on how retailers respond.

Research published in 2025 and 2026 provides evidence of these developments. The implications for 2027 presented below are analytical interpretations of that evidence, rather than confirmed forecasts of retail performance.

CONSUMER TREND / 01

Consumers’ Search for Better Value Could Intensify Competition

47% OF SURVEYED CONSUMERS CLASSIFIED AS VALUE SEEKERS — DELOITTE

Consumers’ search for value involves assessing whether a purchase justifies its total cost. Price matters, but so do quality, durability, convenience, and service. A cheaper product may offer poor value if it requires frequent replacement, while a more expensive alternative may be worthwhile if it lasts longer.

In its 2026 Consumer Products Industry Global Outlook, Deloitte classifies 47% of surveyed consumers globally as value seekers, including 35% of high-income households. The report associates this behaviour with cost-conscious choices, deal-driven purchases, and willingness to sacrifice convenience to save money [1]. These findings indicate that careful spending extends beyond lower-income consumers.

For retailers in 2027, this could increase the importance of demonstrating value at each purchase. Competitive prices may attract attention, but reliable quality and service can help justify the overall offer.

The effect on profitability requires separate consideration. Promotions can increase sales volumes while reducing gross profit per item. Likewise, a shift towards cheaper products may reduce average basket value even if customer traffic remains stable. Retailers should therefore assess whether sales growth reflects additional demand, price changes, or a different product mix.

CONSUMER TREND / 02

Private Labels Could Become a Stronger Source of Differentiation

67% SAY PRIVATE LABELS MEET THEIR NEEDS AS WELL AS BRANDS — EY

The search for value also changes how consumers evaluate branded and retailer-owned products. According to the March 2025 edition of EY’s Future Consumer Index, which surveyed 20,235 consumers across 26 countries, 67% of respondents believed private-label products met their needs as well as branded alternatives. The same publication reports that 54% purchased branded products only when discounted [2].

Together, these findings suggest that recognised brands cannot rely on familiarity alone to secure purchases. Retailers could respond by developing own-brand ranges that offer clear differences in price, quality, and purpose.

A relevant example is Tesco’s Annual Report 2025, which describes its value proposition through a combination of Aldi Price Match, Low Everyday Prices, and Clubcard Prices, alongside its Finest range [7]. This illustrates how a retailer can address affordability while also serving customers seeking premium products. It does not, by itself, establish that any one element caused higher profitability.

For 2027, the implication is that private labels could help retailers address different budgets within the same store. Their success would nevertheless depend on consistent quality, availability, and cost control. A higher private-label sales share should be evaluated alongside category profitability and repeat purchasing.

CONSUMER TREND / 03

AI-Assisted Shopping Could Change Customer Acquisition

As customers become more deliberate about comparing products, AI could change where those comparisons take place. In State of the Consumer 2026, McKinsey explains that AI-generated answers can reduce clicks through to brand and retailer websites, limiting opportunities to influence customers through conventional search channels [3].

Deloitte’s 2026 Retail Industry Global Outlook adds an industry perspective: half of surveyed retail executives expected the multistep shopping journey to converge into a single AI-driven interaction by 2027 [4]. This is an executive expectation, rather than evidence that the transition will occur across all categories.

An existing company example is Amazon’s Rufus shopping assistant, which Amazon introduced to help customers research products, compare options, and receive recommendations [8]. Its introduction demonstrates an application of AI within retail, but it should not be treated as proof of higher conversion or profitability without supporting performance data.

For retailers in 2027, product specifications, stock information, delivery charges, and return conditions could become increasingly important to digital comparisons. The commercial challenge will be to make the overall offer understandable and dependable.

AI may initially redistribute purchases between retailers rather than increase total consumption. Retailers should therefore examine the quality and profitability of customers acquired through these channels.

CONSUMER TREND / 04

Health Priorities Could Reshape Product Ranges

Changing health priorities could influence what retailers stock and how products are presented. PwC’s August 2026 article, The Rise of Everyday Health, draws on a survey of 21,808 consumers across 27 countries and describes health goals as increasingly embedded in daily routines. It also identifies value for money as the leading consideration when respondents choose products and services supporting those goals [5].

This combination is commercially significant. Interest in wellbeing creates opportunities, but customers still require affordable products with understandable benefits.

For grocery retailers, the opportunity could involve convenient foods that meet specific nutritional preferences. Beauty and pharmacy retailers could benefit from clear information and credible guidance. However, expressed interest does not establish how frequently customers will buy a product.

Retailers should therefore test new ranges by location and customer segment, monitoring repeat purchases, stock turnover, and waste. Shelf expansion should follow demonstrated demand. Adding too many similar products could increase inventory costs without generating sufficient additional sales.

CONSUMER TREND / 05

Selective Spending Could Complicate Inventory Planning

Changing preferences can produce different demand patterns across categories. In State of the Consumer 2026, McKinsey identifies both the experience economy and increasingly resourceful consumers as developments shaping consumer markets [3]. This provides a basis for considering how households may protect selected purchases while reducing others.

For retailers, three behaviours need to be distinguished: buying a cheaper alternative, buying a smaller quantity, and postponing the purchase entirely. Each requires a different response.

A shift towards cheaper alternatives may call for a revised assortment. Lower quantities may require smaller orders. Postponed purchases can leave durable goods retailers holding inventory for longer.

In 2027, planning based only on total revenue could obscure these differences. Retailers should combine sales value with unit volumes, stock ageing, and markdown data. More frequent replenishment could improve flexibility, but its benefits must be weighed against procurement and transport costs.

CONSUMER TREND / 06

Smaller Households Could Increase the Importance of Local Assortments

Longer-term household changes will also shape demand. In Five Consumer Behavior Shifts Reshaping Growth in 2026, BCG identifies solo living as a structural development and emphasises suitable package sizes and reliable availability for consumers living alone [6].

This suggests that uniform product ranges may serve some locations less effectively than others. An urban store with many single-person households could require a different mix of portions and package sizes from a suburban store serving larger families.

Smaller packs can reduce the amount customers spend per purchase and limit household waste. However, they may also increase packaging and handling costs per unit. Retailers will need to assess whether the additional demand compensates for these costs.

For 2027, the opportunity lies in matching products to local shopping needs. Store-level sales and customer data could help retailers determine where smaller formats, compact products, or convenience services are commercially justified.

CONSUMER TREND / 07

Personalisation Could Support Retention When Benefits Are Clear

Personalisation offers another way to address differences in consumer needs. However, the relationship between data use and trust requires attention.

In The Rise of Everyday Health, PwC reports that willingness to share detailed health information varies according to the organisation receiving it [5]. This finding concerns health data specifically; its relevance to wider retail personalisation is an interpretation rather than a direct finding about all customer information.

A retailer could use purchase history to offer relevant replenishment reminders or promotions, provided customers understand and accept the arrangement. The benefit should be visible, such as saving time or making suitable products easier to find.

Commercial evaluation is equally important. More coupon redemptions do not necessarily indicate greater loyalty. Some discounts may subsidise purchases that would have occurred anyway. Retailers should assess incremental gross profit and repeat purchasing to determine whether personalisation creates additional value.

INDUSTRY IMPLICATIONS / 08

Different Retail Subsectors Will Face Different Effects

The implications of these trends will vary across retail categories. Grocery retailers, discretionary goods retailers, and digital platforms face different purchasing patterns, inventory requirements, and margin pressures.

Retailers should therefore evaluate consumer trends at the category level rather than assume that every trend will have the same commercial effect across the industry.

ECONOMIC OUTLOOK / 09

Three Scenarios for 2027

Retailers should also consider how different economic conditions could alter these developments.

Scenario Consumer response Retail consequence
Real incomes improve Some postponed purchases resume Recovery in selected discretionary categories
Household budget pressure persists Continued demand for affordable alternatives and deals Stronger price competition and changes in product mix
New cost shocks emerge Essentials absorb more household spending Pressure on discretionary demand and retailer margins

These scenarios provide a framework for planning rather than predictions of which outcome will occur.

Final Thoughts

Retailers’ performance in 2027 will depend on converting customer relevance into profitable sales. Competitive prices, differentiated products, useful technology, and appropriate assortments can support demand. Their contribution to earnings will depend on inventory discipline, fulfilment costs, and customers’ willingness to return without repeated discounts.
