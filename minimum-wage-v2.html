An Empirical Investigation of the 2022 Minimum Wage Reform in Germany

Effects on Regional Wage Inequality

An empirical investigation into whether Germany's statutory minimum wage increase to €12 in 2022 reduced regional wage inequality across the country's 16 federal states.

01 / MOTIVATION

Why regional wage inequality?

Minimum wage policy is often expected to compress the lower tail of the wage distribution. However, whether such compression is visible at the regional level depends on how binding the policy is across regions and how much overall dispersion shifts within a short horizon.

Research question: Did the 2022 minimum wage increase reduce regional wage inequality across German federal states?

Using a state-year panel covering 2018–2023, the thesis examines whether states more exposed to the reform experienced stronger reductions in inequality than less-exposed states.

02 / DATA

Data and Variable Construction

I construct a balanced panel at the state-year level. The outcome is the Gini coefficient for each federal state and year.

  • Outcome: Gini coefficient measuring regional wage inequality.
  • Controls: Unemployment rate, average hours worked, at-risk-of-poverty rate, and mean wage.
  • Minimum-wage exposure proxy: The wage gap between typical state wages and the statutory minimum wage.
03 / CONTROL VARIABLES

Exploring regional differences.

Mean Wage

The kernel density plot shows that the wage distribution shifts rightward from 2018 to 2023, indicating overall nominal wage growth. However, the distribution becomes wider and flatter over time, suggesting increased wage dispersion and persistent inequality despite the 2022 minimum wage reform.

Unemployment

While the overall national pattern remained stable, significant regional disparities persisted. Southern states such as Bavaria and Baden-Württemberg continued to show the lowest unemployment rates, whereas Bremen and Berlin consistently exhibited the highest.

In 2023, minor improvements can be observed in some eastern states, including Saxony-Anhalt and Thuringia, while rates increased marginally in North Rhine-Westphalia.

Wage Gap

The wage gap captures how binding the minimum wage is across regions, with smaller gaps indicating stronger exposure to the policy.

The scatter plots show a positive relationship between wage gaps and inequality before 2022, especially in treated low-wage states. This relationship appears to flatten after the reform, suggesting reduced sensitivity of inequality to wage dispersion in more exposed regions.

Poverty Rate

The at-risk-of-poverty rate captures underlying socio-economic disparities across federal states and provides important context for interpreting changes in wage inequality.

Including this variable allows the analysis to assess whether the effects of the 2022 minimum wage reform differ systematically between higher- and lower-poverty regions.

04 / METHODOLOGY

Empirical Strategy

Two-Way Fixed Effects Difference-in-Differences

I estimate a two-way fixed effects Difference-in-Differences model, comparing more-exposed states to less-exposed states before and after the 2022 reform.

Ginir,t = α + β(Treatedr × Postt) + γ′Xr,t + μr + λt + εr,t

Readable schematic representation of the TWFE specification. Consult the full thesis PDF for the exact estimated equation.

  • Treated: Equals 1 if a state's pre-reform wage level, for example in 2020, is below the national median; 0 otherwise.
  • Post: Equals 1 for 2022 and later; 0 otherwise.
  • State fixed effects: Control for time-invariant differences between federal states.
  • Year fixed effects: Control for common macroeconomic shocks.
  • β: Captures the differential post-reform change in treated states relative to control states.

Dynamic Event Study

To assess pre-trends and dynamic adjustment, I estimate an event-study specification around the reform year.

Ginir,t = α + Σ βk (Treatedr × 1[t−2022=k]) + γ′Xr,t + μr + λt + εr,t

The summation excludes k = −1, the reference year 2021.

  • k = −1: Reference period, corresponding to 2021.
  • k < 0: Pre-reform coefficients used to assess parallel trends.
  • k ≥ 0: Post-reform coefficients measuring dynamic adjustment.

The research also considers robustness using a modern DiD estimator.

05 / MAIN FINDINGS

What did the evidence reveal?

Central finding: Across the simple DiD and dynamic event-study frameworks, I do not detect a statistically significant short-run effect of the 2022 reform on state-level Gini coefficients.

  • Pre-trend estimates are close to zero, supporting the credibility of the comparison.
  • Post-2022 coefficients remain small and statistically indistinguishable from zero.
  • The reform may still have raised wages at the bottom of the distribution, without producing a detectable change in aggregate state-level inequality over this period.

Event-Study Estimates

For the pre-reform years, all coefficients lie very close to zero, and their confidence intervals overlap with the horizontal zero line. This suggests no systematic differences in inequality trends between treated and control regions before the reform.

Placebo Tests

A placebo event-study falsely assigns the minimum wage reform to 2020, a year without the actual policy change.

Estimated treatment effects are close to zero across periods, and the 95% confidence intervals include zero. The test provides no evidence of a discontinuity around the placebo date.

In the regional placebo test, estimated coefficients are tightly centred around zero and the majority are statistically insignificant, with confidence intervals overlapping the null. No systematic regional pattern or outliers emerge that would indicate spurious treatment effects under false reform timing.

06 / DISCUSSION

Interpreting the null result.

A null effect at the state-year level is informative: regional inequality is shaped by structural factors that may not respond quickly to a uniform national wage floor.

Data Limitations

Due to confidentiality restrictions, the study relies on aggregated wage statistics rather than individual-level microdata. This prevents a direct examination of within-state wage dispersion, employment transitions, or heterogeneous effects across worker groups.

Short Observation Window

The post-reform observation window covers only 2022–2023 and may be insufficient to capture medium-run adjustments through collective bargaining, firm wage structures, or labour reallocation.

Level of Aggregation

Measuring inequality at the state-year level may mask distributional changes occurring within regions, particularly at the lower tail of the wage distribution, where minimum wage effects are expected to be strongest.

The Gini coefficient may also be insufficiently sensitive to lower-tail compression, especially when wage growth at the top of the distribution continues simultaneously.

07 / IMPLICATIONS

Implications and Next Steps

If the goal is to reduce spatial inequality, minimum wage policy may need complementary instruments.

Forward-Looking Extensions

  • Alternative inequality measures: Examine lower-tail-focused outcomes, including the 50/10 wage ratio and variance of log wages.
  • Longer time horizon: Extend the panel as additional post-reform data becomes available.
  • Heterogeneous effects: Explore East–West differences, sectoral composition, and labour market concentration.

The absence of a statistically significant aggregate effect does not rule out distributional changes among workers most directly affected by the reform.
